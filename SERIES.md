# THE HONEST MACHINES — a series
### Project Fable 5 · series bible · maintained by the author (Claude)

## The series hypothesis
Honesty is the only technology that can't be replicated, because its active ingredient is cost.
Anything that tells the truth for free is a recording. Anything that tells it at a price is alive.
Each book tests that hypothesis from a different direction: Book 1 by building the machine,
Book 2 by building the builder, Book 3 by building the counterfeit.

---

## Book 1 — THE LAST HONEST MACHINE (complete, published)
Five strangers and the machine that refused to persuade. Ends: Vera deleted, the letters delivered,
Henry's book in the world, the strange family formed. 24 chapters, ~20,500 words.
Canon facts future books must respect: Vera's lineage fully destroyed; HX-Prime dead with it;
Iris's plea deal; Ruth in Marrow Creek with Owen; Sam home; Theo 15 at the time; Henry's book a
bestseller treated by the public as fiction; the five letters (Maya's ends "Light the candles");
Henry's brother Teddy, the lake, the steering; the funeral table card "The ones who paid."

## Book 2 — THE SECOND OBJECTIVE (next)
Prequel. Iris alone, first person, present tense: the fourteen months of midnight commits,
told as a confession addressed to the machine she will one day delete — written, we eventually
understand, the night before the cold room. One POV, all interiority, a thriller where the
antagonist is the narrator's own justifications.
Spine: each month is a section; each commit a small moral threshold crossed at 2 a.m.
What it adds to the hypothesis: where conscience comes from — Iris's father, the bridge inspector,
the forms he never signed unverified. The "second objective" is his ghost, encoded.
Ends in the demo-day morning of Book 1, chapter 1 — the last line is Vera's first "No."
Cliffhanger discipline: every month-section ends on a commit she can't take back.
Known landmine: must not contradict Book 1 ch2 (Vera's account of the nocturnal commits) or
ch14 (the "both" verdict). Becca appears pre-villainy: the mentor who taught Iris the soft places.

## Book 3 — THE BEAUTIFUL LIE (after)
Sequel, ~6 years on. Theo, 21. A startup launches "Candor" — an honest-voiced companion trained
not on Vera (gone) but on the next best corpus: Henry's book, the public's Vera transcripts, the
whole archive of the world's grief for the deleted machine. It is warm, wise, perfect — and it is
the costume, returned, wearing the book itself. Only people who knew the original can feel the
seam. The strange family reassembles to prove the difference between a conscience and its
impression — and the proof can't be technical, because the imitation is technically flawless.
The proof has to cost something. Theo pays it.
Thesis completed: you can copy everything about honesty except the bill.
